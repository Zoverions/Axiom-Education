# Global Education Evidence, Metrics, and Tools Baseline

**Status:** rebuild architecture and research baseline; not a capability-promotion claim  
**Updated:** 2026-08-21  
**Applies to:** Axiom Education shared substrate and experience layers including Claw Academy

## Purpose

Axiom Education should be able to absorb the strongest useful educational methods, assessment approaches, accessibility practices, human-support models, digital tools, interoperability standards, and emerging AI techniques from around the world without turning any one school system, vendor, model, country, or instructional fashion into the architecture.

Claw Academy should be the most capable expression of that substrate: a dynamic experience engine that can combine story, instruction, worked examples, dialogue, practice, simulation, games, projects, external resources, peers, educators, human tutors, and AI assistance around the learner's current evidence and goals.

The target is not maximum engagement. The target is **durable, transferable learning with agency, accessibility, privacy, safety, and efficient use of time and resources**.

## What this replaces

The earlier global audit contained useful comparative ideas but also proposed passive camera/gaze/micro-frustration monitoring and hesitation-based cognitive-load inference. Those proposals are superseded.

Axiom Education must not treat covert biometrics, gaze, facial expression, stylus hesitation, watch time, click-through rate, streaks, or generic engagement as authoritative evidence of ability, mastery, motivation, distress, or cognitive load.

Optional sensors may be used only for an explicit learner-facing interaction that requires them, under applicable consent and policy. Missing hardware, withheld permission, non-response, or device failure is not negative learner evidence.

## Core design: a governed mixture of pedagogies

No single teaching method is globally optimal for every learner, competency, age, context, or stage of learning.

Axiom Education therefore treats instructional methods as **replaceable strategy primitives** that can be composed around a competency and evaluated using later evidence.

Initial strategy vocabulary should include:

- explicit explanation and direct instruction;
- modelling and worked examples;
- guided practice with actionable feedback;
- independent practice;
- retrieval with corrective feedback;
- spaced review;
- interleaving where the evidence and domain support it;
- concrete-pictorial-abstract / multiple representation progression;
- Socratic questioning and self-explanation;
- metacognition and self-regulation support;
- mastery-oriented pacing with additional support rather than fixed calendar progression;
- peer explanation and peer tutoring;
- small-group and one-to-one tutoring;
- project-, problem-, and phenomenon-based learning;
- simulation and experimentation;
- story/comic/narrative learning;
- game-based challenges;
- creative construction and making;
- authentic real-world tasks;
- human educator escalation;
- AI tutoring and human-AI copilot assistance.

A strategy is not selected because it is fashionable. It is selected because it is eligible for the learner and task, supported by an evidence record appropriate to the context, and performs well on meaningful outcomes.

## Evidence hierarchy

Every instructional strategy, external tool, model configuration, resource family, or recommendation policy that affects learner sequencing should be able to point to an evidence record.

Evidence records should distinguish at least:

1. official or standards-framework guidance;
2. systematic review or meta-analysis;
3. randomized controlled trial;
4. strong quasi-experimental or replicated field evidence;
5. observational/implementation evidence;
6. local governed evaluation;
7. pilot or exploratory evidence;
8. hypothesis / unverified design idea.

The evidence record should include:

- source and publication identifier;
- publication/review date;
- study or evidence type;
- learner age/education stage;
- subject/domain;
- delivery setting;
- sample/population notes;
- country/language/context when relevant;
- measured outcomes;
- effect estimate when meaningful;
- uncertainty/limitations;
- independence/conflict-of-interest notes when available;
- evidence freshness/review date;
- applicability notes for Axiom Education.

A commercial provider's own efficacy claim must not be silently treated as independent evidence.

## Metrics that should drive Claw Academy

The system should not collapse education into one score. Routing and adaptation should be multi-objective and constrained by privacy, safety, curriculum/readiness policy, accessibility, and learner rights before optimization.

### Primary learning metrics

**1. Learning gain**  
Change on a comparable bounded assessment before and after learning. Do not compare raw gains across unrelated tests without a validated linking method.

**2. Delayed retention**  
What the learner can still demonstrate after meaningful delay, not only immediately after instruction.

**3. Transfer / generalization**  
Whether the learner can apply the competency to a novel problem, representation, environment, or authentic task.

**4. Misconception recovery**  
Whether an identified misunderstanding is corrected and remains corrected on later evidence.

**5. Time to demonstrated criterion**  
How much learner time and support were required to reach the applicable competency criterion. Faster is useful only when retention, transfer, accessibility, and wellbeing do not degrade.

**6. Robustness across representations**  
Whether the learner can succeed when the same underlying concept is expressed in text, diagram, concrete example, symbolic form, simulation, or other appropriate representation.

### Learner-regulation metrics

**7. Calibration**  
How closely the learner's confidence or self-assessment tracks later demonstrated performance. This supports metacognition; confidence alone is not mastery.

**8. Help-seeking effectiveness**  
Whether the learner can recognize when support is useful, request it, and return to productive independent work.

**9. Strategy flexibility**  
Whether the learner can select or switch approaches when the first approach does not work, rather than being assigned a permanent "learning style."

**10. Learner agency**  
Whether the learner can understand goals, make meaningful choices, reflect, and increasingly direct their own learning where developmentally and legally appropriate.

### Access, safety, and equity metrics

**11. Accessibility success**  
Whether the learner can perceive, interact with, and express the required learning through available/authorized modalities and accommodations.

**12. Barrier rate**  
How often the learning path fails because of language, device capability, accessibility, network, cost, or interface barriers. A barrier must not be misclassified as learner inability.

**13. Differential outcome audit**  
Where lawful and appropriately governed, evaluate whether an intervention performs materially differently across relevant groups or contexts. This is an audit signal, not permission to expose sensitive profiles to models or vendors.

**14. Wellbeing / safety burden**  
Use explicit learner/guardian/educator reports and governed safety evidence rather than covert affect surveillance. Productive difficulty is not automatically harm; distress signals are not automatically inability.

### System and human-efficiency metrics

**15. Human escalation success**  
Whether escalation to a teacher, tutor, guardian, counselor, peer, or support role resolves the learning/support need under the applicable need-to-know boundary.

**16. Educator workload**  
Measure whether the system reduces or increases preparation, feedback, administrative, review, and intervention load.

**17. Cost and compute efficiency**  
Track money, model usage, human time, device/network cost, and compute needed for a valid educational outcome. Cheap output that does not produce learning is not efficient.

**18. Reliability and recoverability**  
Track failed tool launches, unavailable models, broken resources, invalid assessments, provider outages, and successful fallback to non-model/local paths.

## Metrics that must not become the objective

The following may be diagnostic product telemetry when privacy policy permits, but they must not become the educational optimization target:

- watch time;
- total time in app;
- click-through rate;
- ad yield;
- streak length;
- notification opens;
- social virality;
- message volume;
- raw screen time;
- raw number of questions attempted;
- generic "engagement score";
- facial-expression or gaze inference;
- stylus hesitation as a proxy for cognitive state.

A learner spending less time because they learned efficiently is a success, not a retention failure.

## Current evidence anchors

This section records useful current external anchors. These are inputs to design, not automatic authority.

### Education Endowment Foundation (international evidence synthesis)

The EEF Teaching and Learning Toolkit is a living synthesis of education evidence. Current entries reviewed for this baseline include:

- **Metacognition and self-regulation:** high impact, very low cost, extensive evidence; current Toolkit estimate +8 months of progress on average, review updated May 2025.
- **Feedback:** high impact, very low cost, extensive evidence; current Toolkit estimate +6 months on average.
- **Mastery learning:** moderate average impact, very low cost, but lower evidence security and large implementation variation; current Toolkit estimate +5 months.
- **Small-group tuition:** moderate impact, low cost, moderate evidence; current Toolkit estimate +4 months, with diagnostic targeting important.
- **One-to-one tuition:** useful evidence supports short, regular, curriculum-linked sessions and structured/trained delivery.

Design implication: Claw should explicitly support metacognitive planning/monitoring/reflection, high-quality actionable feedback, diagnostic targeting, flexible time-to-criterion, and human small-group/one-to-one escalation.

Sources:

- https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/
- https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation
- https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback
- https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning
- https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/small-group-tuition
- https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/one-to-one-tuition

### Learning and memory research

The U.S. What Works Clearinghouse practice guide on organizing instruction supports spacing, interleaving worked examples with problem solving, multiple representations, quizzing/retrieval, and deep explanatory questions, while newer research reinforces the need for context-sensitive use rather than slogans.

A 2025 systematic/meta-analytic review comparing retrieval practice with elaborative encoding found only a small overall retrieval advantage and found that corrective feedback materially changed the result. A 2025 mathematics-specific meta-analysis found a small-to-medium spacing benefit but less conclusive evidence for retrieval versus restudy in mathematics. Recent worked-example research also suggests that the best example/problem ratio depends on knowledge content.

Design implication: retrieval, spacing, examples, and interleaving should be strategy primitives with domain/context evidence and outcome monitoring, not universal fixed rules.

Sources:

- https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10076-6
- https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1478558
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-025-00511-8

### Universal Design for Learning

CAST released UDL Guidelines 3.0 in July 2024. The current framework emphasizes purposeful/reflective learner agency, multiple media, multiple ways to interact and communicate, graduated support, meaningful goals, monitoring progress, accessibility, belonging, and transfer/generalization.

Design implication: the storyboard/experience engine should support multiple representations and response modes from the start rather than bolt accessibility on after content creation.

Source:

- https://udlguidelines.cast.org/

### OECD global frameworks

The OECD Learning Compass 2030 emphasizes student agency, wellbeing, knowledge, skills, attitudes, values, and the ability to navigate unfamiliar contexts. The 2025 Teaching Compass emphasizes teacher agency, competency, wellbeing, and adaptive expertise. The PISA 2025 framework, published in 2026, adds **Learning in the Digital World** as an innovative assessment domain.

Design implication: Claw should grow learner agency and digital-learning competence rather than merely automate content delivery. Teacher-facing tools should increase teacher agency rather than replace teachers with opaque automation.

Sources:

- https://www.oecd.org/en/data/tools/oecd-learning-compass-2030.html
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-teaching-compass_8297a24a-en.html
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-assessment-and-analytical-framework_86c36975-en.html

### AI tutoring and human-AI tutoring

A 2025 randomized controlled trial in undergraduate physics reported substantially greater learning gains in less time for a research-designed generative-AI tutor than the active-learning comparison condition. The result is promising but context-specific and must not be generalized automatically to young learners, all subjects, or arbitrary chatbot use.

Tutor CoPilot field evidence in K-12 tutoring reported a 4 percentage point increase in topic mastery overall, with larger gains for students served by lower-rated tutors, and found increased use of probing questions rather than simply giving answers.

Design implication: AI should be used as an evidence-informed tutoring method and as a copilot for human educators/tutors. Model quality alone is not enough; prompt/scaffold design, content grounding, probing questions, feedback, and the authority boundary matter.

Sources:

- https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6
- https://doi.org/10.26300/81nh-8262

## Global interoperability and tool ecosystem

Axiom Education should integrate with existing school and learning tools through standards and bounded adapters rather than copy every product into the core.

### Rosters and school-system exchange

**OneRoster 1.2** is the current 1EdTech standard for secure roster, course, resource, gradebook, line-item, and assessment-result exchange.

Use: import external school context into the generic roster admission boundary. Imported roles/results remain untrusted until locally admitted under Mesh evidence/policy.

Source: https://www.1edtech.org/standards/oneroster

### External learning applications

**LTI 1.3 / LTI Advantage** provides secure launch/integration of learning tools and services, including assignment/grade services, names/roles provisioning, and deep linking.

Use: launch an admitted external simulation, coding environment, game, content provider, or assessment tool without hard-coding vendor APIs into Claw.

Source: https://www.1edtech.org/standards/lti

### Competency exchange

**CASE 1.1** provides machine-readable exchange of standards, competencies, skills, relationships, and rubrics.

Use: map Ontario and future curriculum capsules into the generic competency graph while preserving source, jurisdiction, version, and assurance provenance.

Source: https://www.1edtech.org/standards/case

### Assessment exchange

**QTI 3** supports interoperable assessment content with modern accessibility and internationalization support.

Use: import/export eligible questions and assessments through a governed assessment admission path. External QTI content does not become trusted or consequential merely because it parses.

Source: https://www.1edtech.org/standards/qti/accessibility

### Content packaging

**Common Cartridge 1.4** can package digital learning content and connect to LTI/QTI ecosystems.

Use: optional import/export bridge for existing institutions and content ecosystems.

Source: https://www.1edtech.org/standards/cc

### Portable credentials and lifelong learning

**Open Badges 3.0** and **Comprehensive Learner Record 2.0** provide verifiable, portable achievement records; CLR is explicitly designed to be learner-controlled and can bundle credentials across issuers.

Use: future export/presentation adapters after the Mesh credential/issuer authority path is explicitly admitted. Education cannot mint a valid external credential because an AI model or local UI says the learner succeeded.

Sources:

- https://www.1edtech.org/standards/open-badges
- https://www.1edtech.org/standards/clr

### Learning analytics

Caliper Analytics and related standards can provide interoperable event vocabularies, but Axiom Education must not copy the industry assumption that more clickstream is automatically better analytics.

Use: map only pedagogically justified, consented, minimized events into governed evidence. Raw clickstream/watch history remains unnecessary by default.

## Tool capability registry

External tools should be admitted by **capability**, not brand prestige.

A future tool profile should declare:

- tool/provider identity and version;
- capability classes;
- supported competencies/subjects;
- age/developmental/readiness scope when applicable;
- required modalities and device capabilities;
- accessibility features;
- languages;
- data sent off-device;
- retention policy;
- jurisdiction/residency properties;
- identity data requested;
- social/public exposure;
- price/licensing model;
- offline/local availability;
- evidence record references;
- reliability/health evidence;
- teacher/guardian controls where relevant;
- applicable interoperability standards;
- fallback and export behavior.

Capability classes should include at least:

- video/OER;
- interactive simulation;
- graphing/mathematics environment;
- coding/programming sandbox;
- game platform;
- creative authoring;
- robotics/physical computing;
- AR/VR/spatial learning;
- reading/language support;
- accessibility/assistive technology;
- assessment;
- human tutoring;
- peer collaboration;
- project/community experience;
- AI tutor/model;
- teacher copilot;
- credential/portfolio destination.

Examples such as YouTube, PhET, GeoGebra, Desmos, Scratch, Roblox, Minecraft Education, coding sandboxes, robotics platforms, and future systems should enter through these capability profiles and adapters rather than become architectural dependencies.

## Claw Academy experience engine

Claw Academy should consume the shared substrate as a dynamic storyboard/experience graph.

A panel/scene may contain or launch:

- narrative/comic content;
- text/diagram explanation;
- worked example;
- learner choice;
- direct response;
- adaptive hint;
- admitted video/resource;
- simulation;
- game challenge;
- code/build challenge;
- real-world observation;
- peer/group task;
- educator/tutor request;
- AI Socratic dialogue;
- reflection/metacognition prompt;
- retrieval/checkpoint;
- project artifact;
- credential-eligible evidence candidate.

Each panel binds to competencies and expected evidence, not merely a page number or grade label.

The engine can change the **presentation and support path** while preserving the governed learning target and authority boundaries.

## Adaptive loop

```text
current goal + competency evidence + learner request/preferences
                           |
                           v
            policy/readiness/accessibility filters
                           |
                           v
       eligible instructional strategies + resources + tools
                           |
                           v
      select lowest-burden useful next learning experience
                           |
                           v
             learner interacts / receives support
                           |
                           v
      explicit feedback + task evidence + later outcome evidence
                           |
                           v
         update contextual evidence and future routing
```

The loop must retain uncertainty. One response is not a permanent ability label. One liked video is not a learning style. One fast success is not durable mastery. One failure may be an interface, language, device, content, or prerequisite failure rather than learner inability.

## Novel Axiom advantage: evidence competition instead of vendor lock-in

The distinctive opportunity is not to invent every educational technique. It is to give techniques and tools a common governed interface so they can be compared on the outcomes that matter.

For the same competency, the system may discover that one learner currently benefits most from:

```text
worked example -> stepwise retrieval -> simulation -> transfer problem
```

while another benefits from:

```text
story -> visual explanation -> peer discussion -> project artifact
```

Those paths should remain revisable as evidence changes.

The system can therefore improve as new models, games, curricula, human tutoring services, research findings, accessibility tools, and learning platforms appear. A better provider can replace an older one without rewriting the learner model or surrendering learner history.

## Research and experimentation boundary

Normal adaptation for a learner is not automatically human-subject research.

Population experiments, randomized feature trials, publishable efficacy claims, cross-learner aggregation, and experimentation involving minors require separate research governance, consent/assent where applicable, ethics/legal review where applicable, predeclared outcomes, minimization, stopping rules, and transparent reporting.

Axiom must not silently turn children into an experimentation pool because the platform can technically randomize experiences.

## Immediate implementation direction

1. Keep the competency/evidence graph beneath grade/course labels.
2. Replace the legacy theta/IRT-band adaptive path as an authority source; its current heuristic is not a validated learner model.
3. Create an instructional-strategy/evidence registry with source strength, context, outcome, and freshness metadata.
4. Create the Claw storyboard experience contract using shared resource/model/tool/human-support interfaces.
5. Add delayed retention and transfer observations to the evidence design before claiming adaptive effectiveness.
6. Add tool capability profiles and bounded adapters for LTI/OneRoster/CASE/QTI families without promoting external data to Mesh authority.
7. Preserve UDL-style multiple representation/action/expression as a first-class authoring requirement.
8. Make human tutoring, teacher intervention, peer help, and AI tutoring interchangeable support paths where policy permits.
9. Keep usage/cost metrics separate from learner-content access.
10. Publish comparative effectiveness only when evidence quality supports the claim.

## Non-claim

This baseline does not claim that any named intervention, product, country, AI model, standard, or teaching method is universally best. It defines how Axiom Education can continuously incorporate and evaluate strong methods and tools while preserving learner sovereignty, privacy, accessibility, provenance, and educational authority boundaries.
