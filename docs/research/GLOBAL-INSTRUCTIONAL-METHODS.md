# Global Instructional Methods Baseline

**Status:** instructional design policy
**Scope:** high-school course production, beginning with Ontario MTH1W
**Reviewed:** 2026-08-01

## Decision

Axiom Education will teach a common, curriculum-defined outcome through more
than one mathematically valid route. Lessons must combine clear explanation,
worked examples, connected representations, guided practice, independent
practice, retrieval, and reflection. A learner may compare routes and choose a
useful strategy for the current task.

The platform must not assign students to fixed visual, auditory, reading, or
kinaesthetic "learning style" categories. The Education Endowment Foundation's
[current evidence review](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/learning-styles)
finds extremely limited evidence for matching teaching to such labels and
warns that restricting learners to labelled activities may be harmful. Choice
in Axiom Education is therefore a choice of strategy or representation, not a
diagnosis of learner type.

## Comparative assessment

No national system supplies a universal winning method. The useful common
ground is a set of complementary practices that can be tested against the same
learning goal.

| Source or system | Useful emphasis | How Axiom Education uses it | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Australia — [AERO explicit instruction](https://www.edresearch.edu.au/guides-resources/practice-guides/explicit-instruction-practice-guide-full-publication) | Clear goals, small steps, modelling, worked examples, checks for understanding, and gradual removal of support | Teach new procedures explicitly before asking for independent work | Explicit teaching is a starting structure, not a script that removes reasoning or discussion |
| Singapore — [secondary mathematics syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/secondary/syllabuses/maths/2020-express_na-maths_syllabuses.ashx) | Problem solving integrates concepts, skills, processes, metacognition, and attitudes; students encounter routine and non-routine problems and modelling | Connect procedures to problem solving, explanation, modelling, and monitoring | Do not reduce a national framework to a branded trick or claim it works identically in every context |
| Japan — [MEXT Courses of Study](https://www.mext.go.jp/en/policy/education/elsec/title02/detail02/1373859.htm) | Common national standards balance foundational knowledge and skills with thinking, decision-making, and expression | Preserve base coverage while asking students to justify, compare, and communicate methods | Standards describe aims; they do not by themselves prove the effect of a particular classroom routine |
| United States — [IES study guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) | Space learning, interleave worked examples with problem solving, connect graphics with verbal descriptions, connect concrete and abstract forms, use retrieval, and ask explanatory questions | Build review schedules and link symbolic, verbal, tabular, graphical, and concrete representations | Apply recommendations in subject context and monitor whether students are actually learning |
| England — [EEF metacognition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation), [mastery learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning), and [collaborative learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/collaborative-learning-approaches) | Explicit plan-monitor-evaluate routines have strong evidence; mastery and structured collaboration are promising but implementation and results vary | Add self-explanation and correction; require prerequisite repair before progression; use carefully structured peer tasks when a classroom path exists | Do not turn mastery into endless lockout or collaboration into unstructured group work |
| CAST — [Universal Design for Learning Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) | Multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression | Offer accessible representations and response modes while holding the learning goal and quality criteria steady | Options must remain mathematically equivalent and must not lower the intended outcome |

## Required lesson contract

Every production lesson must include:

1. one explicit curriculum outcome and prerequisite knowledge;
2. concise direct instruction in manageable steps;
3. at least one fully worked example with visible intermediate reasoning;
4. at least two valid reasoning routes where the subject permits them;
5. at least two connected representations, with an explanation of how they
   express the same idea;
6. a prompt that asks the learner to compare routes, select one, and justify
   the selection for the task;
7. guided practice, independent practice, and deterministic task feedback;
8. later retrieval and mixed review rather than a single same-day performance;
9. a plan-monitor-evaluate or self-explanation prompt; and
10. accessible input, output, and printable/offline alternatives that preserve
    the target outcome.

If only one legitimate method exists for a narrowly defined task, the content
review must record that exception. Artificially different wording does not
count as a second route.

## How effectiveness is judged

Methods are retained or revised using outcome-aligned evidence: correctness on
new problems, explanation quality, delayed retrieval, transfer across
representations, error patterns, and educator review. Speed, clicks, hint use,
or a learner's preference alone must not be interpreted as ability, diagnosis,
motivation, or mastery.

The initial four foundation lessons demonstrate the lesson contract but remain
a preview. Full MTH1W coverage requires reviewed lesson, practice, assessment,
accessibility, and educator evidence for all official expectations recorded in
the coverage ledger.
